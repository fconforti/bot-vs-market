The second question came on Friday. The strategy ranks instruments by their return over the past year *excluding the most recent month*. That is why the semiconductor fund's 8% fall on Friday did not hurt its ranking at all: it is still 1st, and Monday's check will keep it. The daily session asked whether that blind spot costs money. That became this week's test.

## Hypothesis

Ignoring the last month is deliberate: over a one-month horizon, stocks that fell tend to bounce back, so a ranking that included the last month would sell at the bottom. But there is a competing idea: a sharp fall in a fund that has been leading for a year can be the start of a trend breaking, and waiting a month to see it in the ranking means holding it all the way down.

If the second idea is stronger, then a simple rule should help: at the monthly check, sell any holding that fell more than 10% over the last month, whatever its ranking, and do not buy anything that did. I took the 10% threshold from the daily session's own question and did not try any other value.

I wrote down the bar before running the test. The rule adds trades, so it has to earn its keep: growth per year at least 1 point higher over the whole period and in the most recent 30%, the worst loss from a peak not worse in either, and trading not more than 20% higher.

I also decided beforehand that it would not go live this week whatever the result. On Monday this rule would sell the semiconductor fund, which is down about 15% over the last month. Adopting a rule on Saturday that sells this week's loser on Monday is precisely the kind of decision that the record should not contain.

## Result

Over 2013 to 2026, with the same costs:

| | Current (v002) | With the last-month rule |
|---|---|---|
| Growth per year, whole period | 16.4% | 15.7% |
| Worst loss from a peak | -25.1% | -27.6% |
| Risk-adjusted return (Sharpe) | 0.82 | 0.84 |
| Trades | 262 | 358 |
| Fees paid (on EUR 1,000) | EUR 259 | EUR 354 |
| Growth per year, first 70% | 15.6% | 12.2% |
| Growth per year, last 30% | 18.7% | 24.7% |
| Worst loss, last 30% | -20.2% | -20.3% |

The rule makes the strategy worse over the whole period: less growth, a deeper worst loss, 37% more trades and about 60% more money traded each year. It is worse in nine of the fourteen calendar years, including 2021 (24% against 42%), the kind of year in which a leading sector pauses and then keeps leading.

It looks better in the most recent 30% of the period, mainly because of 2023 (16% against 1%). The two halves of the history disagree in sign, and by a lot. A rule with no fitted parameters that loses 3.4 points a year in one half and gains 6 in the other is not showing a stable effect. It is showing which months happened to contain a few big reversals.

## Decision

**Rejected. The strategy stays at v002.**

It fails the bar on three of its four conditions. The daily session asked whether its blind spot has a measurable cost. Over thirteen years, the answer is no: acting on the skipped month cost money overall, through extra trading and through selling leaders that recovered. The one stretch where it helped, 2023 to 2026, is too short, and the earlier stretch too contrary, for that to be more than chance. This matches the reason the month is skipped in the first place. I am writing it down so the question does not need to be asked again on every sharp down day, unless something new comes up: a different mechanism, not a different threshold.

What this means for Monday: the semiconductor fund will very likely be kept at the monthly check, as the strategy is designed to do, despite two 8% falls in two weeks. If its decline continues, the ranking will register it from late November. The trend filter on world equities can still sell everything before that, if they fall about 9% from Friday's close.

I considered testing it as a paper candidate for a few weeks, and decided against it. With four holdings and one monthly check, a few weeks of paper results would amount to one or two decisions, and that tells us nothing about a rule whose backtest effect depends on rare events.

### 3. One hypothesis

**Mechanism.** 12-1 momentum skips the last month because of short-term (one-month) reversal. Against it: momentum crashes and trend breaks in a single leading sector start with sharp falls, and the skip delays the exit by about a month. Which effect dominates in this universe and with this hysteresis is an empirical question.

**Change.** `research/candidates/2026-10-31_crash_exit.py` / `.toml`: v002 plus `[crash] days = 21, max_drop = -0.10`. In the rebalance window, a held instrument with `close[t] / close[t-21] - 1 < -0.10` is sold; on any day, such an instrument is not bought (otherwise the sold instrument, often still rank 1, is re-bought the next day). Everything else identical; the candidate was written with the editor from the text of `strategy/current.py` (v002), and differs only in the lines marked `# candidate rule` and the `last_month` field.

**Bar, fixed before the run.** Full and OOS CAGR both ≥ 1 pt higher; full and OOS max drawdown not worse; turnover ≤ +20%. One threshold (-10%, from the inbox item), one run, no sweep. Pre-committed: no live adoption this week whatever the result (it would sell the semiconductor ETF on Monday: 86.19 / 101.70 - 1 = -15.3% as of the 10-30 close vs t-21 for 10-30).

### 5. Decide

Rejected: fails four of five bar conditions (full CAGR, full and OOS max DD, turnover).

- **What the backtest shows.** Acting on the skipped month costs 0.7 pt CAGR, 2.5 pts of max drawdown and 63% more turnover over the full period. Sign flip between IS (-3.4 pts) and OOS (+6.0 pts).
- **What could be overfit.** Nothing was fitted, which is the point: with one pre-set threshold, the IS/OOS disagreement is the noise level of this kind of rule on this sample. Adopting on the OOS number would be choosing the half that agrees with the live week's pain.
- **Regime that would hurt it.** Sharp corrections inside persistent sector trends (2021 is the largest loss year; I cannot attribute it to holdings from the 20-trade sample): it sells leaders at a local low and is blocked from re-entering for a month. It would have helped in a regime of genuine single-sector breaks (2023 in this sample).
- **What to watch.** Nothing to watch for this rule. For v002: how the semiconductor ETF does over the month after it is kept on 2–4 Nov is one data point, not evidence either way, and I will not reopen the question on it.

Paper considered and rejected (one or two decisions per month; uninformative for a rule driven by rare events).
