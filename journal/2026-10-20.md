## 7. Decision

**I refuse to trade today. Both orders are declined. No trade files are written and nothing is sent to the broker.**

The orders themselves are what v002 should propose. The ranking does not depend on the bad bar, and the mandate limits would pass (the per-order and per-position caps, the cash reserve, the minimum order). I am refusing because of the conditions under which they would be executed. The rule is that anything that would make a careful human pause is a valid reason. Here there are three such reasons, and all of them point the same way:

1. **The session is running outside the mandate's session time.** `MANDATE.md` and `mandate.toml` set the session at 18:15 Europe/Rome. This one started at about 09:01, one minute after the open. I can see no reason for it in the repository. The strategy assumes an order placed after the close, near that day's close, and the cost model says so in writing ("execution at the same day's close plus half the spread, which is closer to an 18:15 fill on EIX"). The guardrails do not check the time of day, so nothing in code would stop this. That is all the more reason not to rely on code to stop it.
2. **The spread on the rank-1 instrument is six times the cost model.** The VanEck Semiconductor ETF is quoted at 88.76 / 90.12, a spread of 1.52%. Yesterday afternoon it was 0.02%, and the model assumes 0.25%. A market buy pays the ask. Two shares at 90.12 cost EUR 180.24, about EUR 1.36 more than the same shares at the mid. In money that is small. But for the account's first live order, it means buying at a price set while the US market, where the ETF's holdings trade, is closed. The Energy ETF's spread (0.48%) is also about twice the model.
3. **The last bar is an intraday price, not a close (step 3).** It does not change the ranking or the trend filter today. But both share counts are computed from it, and it is about 1% above the broker's own reference for the semiconductor ETF. The strategy is specified on daily closes. Today it would run on a 16:40 price from yesterday and execute at a 09:00 price from today, and neither is what the backtest assumes.

What refusing costs: the two entries are delayed by at least one trading day. For a strategy that ranks on 12-month momentum and expects to hold for months, that is noise. There is no position to protect and no exit pending. What trading would risk is smaller in euros. But the first real-money order would be placed under conditions the mandate, the strategy and the cost model all rule out, and the rules say to prefer doing nothing over doing something unjustified.

Partial acceptance: not justified. Reason 1 applies to both orders equally. The Energy ETF's spread is less extreme, but reason 3 also applies to it (cache 0.4% above the broker reference).
